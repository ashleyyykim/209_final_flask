Project Overview

Every time a superstar's team loses in the playoffs, the discourse writes itself. Did he choke, or did his supporting cast no-show? It's one of the oldest arguments in basketball, and it gets rehashed every single postseason for whichever star's team went home early that year. This project checks whether an argument like that actually holds up in the numbers, using PIE (Player Impact Estimate), the NBA's own measure of a player's overall contribution to a game. We pulled player, team, and series-level data from the NBA Stats API across multiple seasons and built every hypothesis on this site around that one shared metric.

We approach the question at three scales, each one narrowing the lens further:

  1. Elite players. Do the league's highest regular-season performers stay elite once the playoffs begin?
  2. Role players. Do teams whose supporting cast holds or raises its PIE in the playoffs tend to win more games?
  3. The defining star. Does each team's top player perform better in the series his team wins than in the ones it loses?

The same question, asked at three magnifications.

What is PIE?

A single number for how much of a game one player was responsible for.

PIE folds a player's full box score, both ends of the floor, into one percentage. Every chart here uses it instead of raw stats like points, since it accounts for defense and playmaking too, not just scoring.

03

Do Stars Rise to the Moment in Playoff Wins?

The intuition is simple: when a team wins a playoff series, its star must have risen to the occasion. This would mean outplaying his regular-season self while the losing team's star faltered. This visualization sets out to explore whether the defining star on each team (its highest regular-season PIE player) really does perform better, relative to his own regular season, in series his team wins versus loses during the playoffs.
